## 1. Mathematical Definitions of System Delay Metrics

### Question 1: What exactly does the paper mean by "all-task completion delay"?
- **Paper Definition**: In Section III-A and Section VI-B, "all-task completion delay" ($Time_{total}$) is defined as the total accumulated completion time across all tasks $k \in \{1, \dots, |m_i^t|\}$ generated by all End Devices $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ over the entire simulation horizon of $K=300$ seconds:
  $$Time_{total} = \sum_{t=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{k=1}^{|m_i^t|} Time_{i,k}^t$$

### Question 2: What exactly does our code calculate as `all_task_delay`?
- **Code Definition**: In [`evaluate.py`](file:///c:/Users/JADAV%20YUVARAJ/OneDrive/Desktop/lrma-iot-offloading/evaluate.py#L117) and [`run_experiments.py`](file:///c:/Users/JADAV%20YUVARAJ/OneDrive/Desktop/lrma-iot-offloading/run_experiments.py#L90), `all_task_delay` is calculated as:
  ```python
  all_task_completion_delay = float(np.sum(task_delays))
  ```
  where `task_delays` is the list containing $Time_{i,k}^t$ for every generated task over the simulation slots.

### Question 3: What exactly does the paper mean by "average task completion delay"?
- **Paper Definition**: In Section VI-B, "average task completion delay" ($Time_{avg}$) is defined as the mean completion time per task across all $M_{total}$ generated tasks:
  $$Time_{avg} = \frac{1}{M_{total}} \sum_{t=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{k=1}^{|m_i^t|} Time_{i,k}^t = \frac{Time_{total}}{M_{total}}$$

### Question 4: What exactly does our code calculate as `average_delay`?
- **Code Definition**: In [`evaluate.py`](file:///c:/Users/JADAV%20YUVARAJ/OneDrive/Desktop/lrma-iot-offloading/evaluate.py#L118), `avg_task_delay` is calculated as:
  ```python
  avg_task_completion_delay = float(np.mean(task_delays))
  ```

### Question 5: Are the two definitions mathematically identical?
- **Answer**: **YES**. Both the paper and our codebase define `all_task_delay` as the sum of completion delays $\sum Time_{i,k}^t$ and `avg_task_delay` as the arithmetic mean $\frac{1}{M_{total}} \sum Time_{i,k}^t$.

## 4. Offloading Decision Analysis & Mathematical Proof

### Question 20: Compare offloading decision distribution
- **Paper Target**: Peak offloading ratio $\approx 49.94\%$ ($\sim 50\%$).
- **Our Reproduction Result**: Offloading ratio $\approx 98.40\%$.

### Question 21: Determine mathematically why our RL agent prefers offloading almost all tasks

#### Mathematical Proof of Offloading Dominance

Consider an arbitrary task $k$ with CPU demand $C$, size $size = \rho C$, generated by ED $i$:

1. **Local Execution Cost**:
   - Delay: $Time_{local} = D^{ED} + W^{ED} = \frac{C}{f_{local}} + W^{ED} = \frac{9.74 \times 10^9}{1.0 \times 10^9} + W^{ED} = 4.8713 \text{ s} + W^{ED}$
   - Energy: $E_{local} = 0.5 \times \frac{size}{8 \times 10^6} \text{ Joules}$

2. **Offloading Cost (MES Execution)**:
   - Transmission Delay: $t_{trans} = \frac{size}{v} = \frac{97.43 \times 10^6 \text{ bits}}{179.37 \times 10^6 \text{ bps}} = 0.5703 \text{ s}$
   - MES Processing Delay: $D^{BS} = \frac{C}{f_{es}} = \frac{9.74 \times 10^9}{10.0 \times 10^9} = 0.9743 \text{ s}$
   - Total Offloading Delay (without queue wait):
     $$Time_{offload} = t_{trans} + D^{BS} + W^{BS} = 0.5703 + 0.9743 + W^{BS} = 1.5446 \text{ s} + W^{BS}$$
   - Transmission Energy: $E_{offload} = 0.2 \times \frac{size}{8 \times 10^6} \text{ Joules}$

3. **Comparison**:
   $$\Delta Delay = Time_{local} - Time_{offload} = 4.8713\text{ s} - 1.5446\text{ s} = 3.3267 \text{ s} \quad (\text{Offloading is } \mathbf{3.15\times} \text{ faster!})$$
   $$\Delta Energy = E_{local} - E_{offload} = (0.5 - 0.2) \times \text{size} = 0.3 \times \text{size} > 0 \quad (\text{Offloading is } \mathbf{2.5\times} \text{ more energy efficient!})$$

#### Conclusion & Root Cause
Because MES CPU clock frequency ($10$ GHz) is $10\times$ faster than local ED CPU clock frequency ($1$ GHz) while wireless transmission bandwidth ($179$ Mbps) makes transmission delay ($0.57$ s) tiny, **offloading is strictly dominant in both delay and energy** whenever MES queues are not severely backlogged ($W^{BS} < 3.32$ s).

The RL agent naturally learns that offloading yields significantly higher Lyapunov rewards (Eq. 38–40), resulting in an offloading ratio of **$98.40\%$**. In the paper, additional unstated background MES queue congestion or interference raised $W^{BS}$ above $3.32$ s, balancing offloading at $\sim 50\%$.
